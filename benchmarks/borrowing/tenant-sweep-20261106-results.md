# Tenant-count comparison on the GPU server

Increasing tenant count fills more trainer time, but does not eliminate idle windows in this workload. Borrowing only activates at eight tenants and does not continue after the initial minute. This run does not establish a meaningful performance benefit from the current policy.

## Setup

Measured on 2026-11-06 using two GPUs, Qwen3.5-4B, and the HF training backend. Each of 1, 2, 4, and 8 tenants runs eight concurrent four-turn coding trajectories, then trains and publishes an adapter, twice. Tenant starts are staggered by five seconds. Tool feedback is generated by isolated CPU containers. Both arms retain the same shared frozen-base layout; borrowing is disabled or enabled through admission settings.

Dedicated sampler capacity is 32, borrowed-GPU sampler capacity is four, maximum output is 1,536 tokens, and the borrowing budget is 35 seconds with a 1.5 safety factor. Inference uses eager execution and Triton GDN prefill. Training uses FLA with TileLang backward and the reference causal-convolution fallback.

Each count has one ordered off/on pair. Warmup is excluded from measurement and adapter/optimizer state is restored afterward. Initial adapter fingerprints match within every pair. The already-downloaded pinned checkpoint was reused; future downloads must use ModelScope. No GPU experiments were repeated after a valid arm completed.

## Results

Each paired cell is **borrowing off / on**. GPU utilization is the time-weighted NVML utilization reading, not SM occupancy. “No ready training” means the scheduler has neither active nor pending training work; it can overlap borrowed sampling.

At eight tenants:

- The baseline has 492.2 seconds with no ready training work and queued sampling. The on arm still has 484.3 seconds. Requests are available; insufficient sampling demand is not the explanation here.
- Elapsed time is 1,018.4 / 1,007.4 seconds (1.1% lower with borrowing). Sampling p95 is 110.3 / 108.8 seconds. Training-update p95 is 192.5 / 194.0 seconds. One pair cannot separate these small differences from run variation.
- Eight borrowed requests finish: four cold probes followed by four estimated admissions. Shared sampling is active for 43.9 seconds. There are zero admissions after the first update.
- Probes are admitted at 20.0 seconds and finish at 32.3–32.5 seconds. The next four requests are admitted at 32.5 seconds and finish at 60.7–63.9 seconds. Their admission estimate was about 15.2 seconds; actual service was about 28.2–31.4 seconds.
- After these observations, example service estimates of 31–45 seconds become 47–68 seconds of occupied time after the safety margin and switching cost, above the 35-second budget. The duration gate rejects 5,348 candidate evaluations; this is a count of evaluations, not distinct requests. The saved trace also shows a few cost rejections, so budget is not the only gate for every candidate.
- Total training drain is below 0.1 ms in both arms. The small training p95 increase is not explained by waiting for borrowed sampling. Ready training queueing and service time must be distinguished from borrowing delays.

At one, two, and four tenants, there are no borrowed requests. Eight trajectories per tenant yield at most 8, 16, and 32 simultaneous sampling requests, which fit on the dedicated sampler. These arms establish the no-backlog controls; they cannot demonstrate a borrowing speedup.

## What to do next

The workload creates a real borrowing opportunity at eight tenants, but the fixed interruption budget prevents most of it from being used. Simply adding tenants does not guarantee a continuously busy trainer. However, this is a short workload with similarly structured clients, not evidence about a large, diverse steady-state service.

The next design question is how to authorize longer safe borrowing windows. Client signals about outstanding rollout groups and when training can next be submitted could expose such windows without implementing partial rollouts first. A larger budget alone trades against possible training delays and would need a separate comparison. The early underestimate also remains a reason to inspect prediction accuracy by request shape before trusting automatic calibration. No readiness API, partial-rollout support, policy change, or further GPU run was added here.

## Validation and limits

All eight arms pass: 1,920 sampling requests, 60 training updates, two updates per tenant, matching initial adapters, unchanged frozen weights, and zero copied base parameters in the borrowed-GPU sampler. None of the requests hits the output limit. The CPU workload tests passed (13 tests); the standalone CUDA 13 GDN forward/backward check produced finite outputs and gradients. FSDP code and sharding were not changed, and this HF experiment does not validate FSDP performance.

Task success is low (1–3 completed trajectories per arm); all trajectories still execute their four turns and feed training. These results validate scheduling and execution, not learning quality. There are only two updates per tenant, one base model, and one ordered pair per count. The reference convolution fallback and eager inference also limit transfer to a fully optimized deployment. Initialization failures and fixes are retained separately and are not included in measured timing.
